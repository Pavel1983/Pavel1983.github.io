Privacy Policy — Times Tables Practice

Last updated: 8 October 2026

This is the full privacy notice for Times Tables Practice. The same page is linked from the first-run screen in the app and is the URL we use on the Google Play store listing. The site is served over HTTPS.

This notice describes what the game stores, what starts after you tap Got it, and which third parties then process data. It is written in plain language so a parent or guardian can see the practices before ads and analytics start.

Who we are

This game is made by Pavel Totolin. Times Tables Practice is an educational multiplication-table app for children. This notice applies to anyone who uses the game on Google Play, wherever they are.

We do not create an account. We do not ask for a name, email, or phone number.

What the game stores locally

On this device the game may keep:

  • quiz results, weak facts, progress, and achievements
  • language and sound preference

We do not send individual quiz answers or facts such as 7×8. Uninstalling the app or clearing app data deletes this local data.

What happens when you tap Got it

The first-run screen is a short notice, not parental consent under COPPA and not an account signup. It includes a button that opens this page.

Until you tap Got it, the game does not start analytics or ads.

If you tap Got it, analytics and advertising are enabled as described below. You can keep using the game without creating an account.

If you leave the app before tapping Got it, we do not start analytics or ads.

Analytics

After Got it, Unity Analytics collects:

  • a random identifier created by Unity to distinguish installations (not your name)
  • app version, language, and whether sound is on
  • which screen is open (menu, number_select, and similar)
  • session settings (mode, difficulty, timed, diagonal, selected tables as numbers like 1,2,5)
  • session summary: correct / wrong counts, duration, score, whether the timer ran out, new achievements

Unity’s SDK may also receive standard device fields (for example platform and a coarse country).

We use this to see how the game is used and to fix it. We do not use it to profile a child across other apps.

Unity’s own practices: https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy

Advertising

After Got it, the app shows a single banner ad from Google AdMob. AdMob is the only advertising SDK in the app, and it is part of Google Play’s Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program. We do not use ad mediation or other ad networks.

How ads are configured for children

Every ad request is configured for children:

  • child-directed treatment is on (COPPA)
  • requests are marked as under the age of consent (GDPR and similar laws)
  • the maximum ad content rating is G (suitable for general audiences, including young children)

With these settings Google does not show personalized or interest-based ads, does not use remarketing, and limits the data it uses to what is needed to serve and measure a contextual ad and to prevent fraud.

IP address

Ad requests travel over the internet. The device’s IP address is included in those requests so Google can deliver an ad, choose one suitable for the region, and limit fraud. We do not use IP address to identify a child by name.

Advertising ID

We do not declare or request the Android Advertising ID (AAID) permission. Our Android builds strip com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID. We do not use AAID for remarketing or interest-based advertising. Google may still use other technical device data and app-scoped identifiers needed to serve a contextual, child-directed banner, cap how often it is shown, and prevent fraud.

Location

We do not request location permission. We do not collect precise GPS location. A coarse region may still be inferred from IP address by Google or Unity.

We do not send quiz answers, names, or emails to Google or any ad partner.

How Google uses data from apps that show its ads: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Google’s privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Children and child-directed use

This game is designed for children learning the times tables. We do not knowingly collect names, email addresses, phone numbers, or other information intended to directly contact a child.

Ads shown after Got it are requested as child-directed. We still do not treat the Got it tap as verified parental consent.

What we do not collect

  • name, email, phone, or login
  • individual quiz answers or facts
  • microphone, camera, contacts
  • precise GPS location
  • Android Advertising ID (we do not request or declare it)

Who receives data

After Got it, analytics and ads are processed by:

  • Unity (analytics) — privacy policy
  • Google (AdMob advertising) — privacy policy

Those parties may process data on their own terms. This notice does not replace their policies.

Where this notice appears

  • this HTTPS page: https://paveltotolin.dev/times-tables-practice/privacy.html
  • inside the app, from the first-run screen (opens this page)
  • the Google Play store listing privacy-policy field (same URL)

International processing

These providers may process data on servers in countries other than the one where you play, according to their own policies and applicable contractual and technical safeguards.

Security

Analytics and ad requests use encrypted internet connections (HTTPS). Local game progress stays on the device. We do not operate a player account server.

Retention

Local game data is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear app data. There is no account to delete on our side.

Data already collected by Unity or Google may remain with those providers under their own policies and procedures. Clearing the app does not erase copies they already hold.

Your privacy rights

You (or a parent / guardian) may contact us to ask about personal data processed for this app, request rights that apply where you live, or ask for help with a privacy request.

If you are in the Philippines, that includes rights under the Data Privacy Act of 2012. You may also complain to the National Privacy Commission. In other countries, a local data-protection authority may apply.

Uninstalling the app or clearing app data stops local collection on this device. It does not by itself delete data already held by Unity or Google.
